01 · The problem

When a weapon comes out, you have about a second. Everything people carry for protection asks them to reach, aim and escalate inside that second. Wisp asks for one press, then gets you out.

02 · The system

Worn like nothing. Ready in a second.

Three parts that work as one: a harness you forget you are wearing, a drone the size of your hand, and flight software that keeps light in the attacker's eyes while you leave.

Silk harness · W1
Silk

Two straps. Nothing else.

Silk is worn like the lightest backpack you own: two padded straps over the shoulders, a sternum strap across the chest and a thin carbon plate between the shoulder blades. Wisp docks flat on the plate and charges there.

The trigger is the clasp on the sternum strap, right where your hand already is.

  • 150 g
  • Carbon back plate
  • 38 mm webbing
  • Chest clasp trigger
  • Charges while worn
Wisp · drag to rotate
Wisp

A light that flies.

Four guarded rotors in one molded frame, 132 mm across. Two high-output emitters and a camera behind a single smoked sapphire window. Machined motor bells, molded grilles above and below every blade.

Wisp can take a bump from a hand or a wall and keep flying.

  • 120 g
  • 132 mm
  • 1,500 lm
  • 4 min active flight

Orbit

Never where they look.

Orbit is the flight software. It finds the face, holds the beam on the eyes and keeps moving through six patterns, so the drone is never where the attacker expects it.

When the wearer is clear, Wisp breaks off and follows them out.

03 · How it works

One press. Three seconds. Walk away.

Three seconds from the press to light on the attacker's eyes, then the walk away.

  1. 01 · 0.0 s

    Press

    A firm press on the chest clasp arms and releases Wisp. No phone, no app, no looking down.

  2. 02 · 0.7 s

    Launch

    The dock lets go and Wisp lifts off the back, climbing over the shoulder and away from the wearer.

  3. 03 · 2.6 s

    Lock

    The camera finds the face turned toward the wearer and both emitters land on the eyes. Aiming gets very hard.

  4. 04 · 3.2 s

    Orbit

    Wisp circles the head at arm's length and keeps changing its path, so it can't be tracked or swatted. The wearer backs away.

Timings are design targets, measured from the press.

04 · Inside Wisp

Seven parts. Nothing extra.

Scroll to take Wisp apart.

  1. 01CanopyA crowned shell in glass-filled nylon. The ring on top shows charge and state at a glance.
  2. 02OpticsTwo 750-lumen emitters in polished reflectors and a global-shutter camera between them, behind one smoked sapphire window.
  3. 03ComputeA vision processor finds faces and flies the aircraft on board. Wisp needs no phone and no network.
  4. 04RotorsFour 45 mm five-blade rotors on outrunner motors with machined aluminum bells.
  5. 05GuardsMolded grilles above and below every rotor. A finger can't reach a blade.
  6. 06AirframeOne molded frame. The ducts are the structure, which is how the whole aircraft stays at 120 grams.
  7. 07Power and dockA two-cell 550 mAh pack. Magnets and gold pogo contacts hold Wisp to the harness and charge it there.

06 · Principles

Built to de-escalate.

Wisp is a tool for getting away. These rules shape every part of it.

Non-lethal, full stop

No projectiles, no spray, no contact. The only thing Wisp puts on a person is light.

Eye-safe light

Output is set for the closest distance Wisp flies to a face. Dazzle, not damage.

Private by default

Wisp records only while deployed. Clips are encrypted on the device and leave it only if the wearer shares them.

Accountable

Every deployment is logged with time, place and flight path, so what happened can be shown, not argued.
